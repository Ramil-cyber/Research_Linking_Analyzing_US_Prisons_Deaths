Linking and Analyzing Data on Deaths in U.S. Prisons

Overview
This project analyzes and links multiple datasets to provide a transparent, data-driven view of deaths occurring in U.S. correctional facilities. It is part of the Third City Project, which aims to shed light on the hidden data within the U.S. criminal legal system and advocate for the well-being of impacted individuals and communities.

Team
Team Members: Ramil Mammadov, Johnathan Ross, Sam Grossman

Project Leads: Dr. Lauren Brinkley-Rubinstein, David Cloud

Project Manager: Abdul Wasay

Background
The U.S. currently holds between 1.9–2.1 million prisoners.

U.S. ranks 5th globally in prisoners per capita.

Disparities in prison population include:

32% of prisoners are Black

68% are from minority groups

Historical contributors: War on Drugs, mandatory minimums, parole restrictions.

Data Sources
State DOC Press Releases: Names, demographics, causes of death

Bureau of Justice Statistics (BJS): Death records (2015–2019), with standardized codes

Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA): Ongoing data from the Deaths in Custody Reporting Program (DCRA)

Methodology
Merging of federal and state datasets

Data cleaning and validation

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Statistical & machine learning methods (e.g., clustering, regression)

Development of an interactive public map

Building tables on:

Independent oversight agencies

Death reporting laws and compliance by state

Key Findings
Deaths rose by 18% from 2020 to 2023 (≈6.7k deaths in 2023).

Texas reported the highest number of prison deaths (≈3.3k).

Race & Sex Disparities:

Most deaths: White (≈14k), then Black (≈8k)

Male deaths: 55% White, 31% Black

Female deaths: 63% White, 23% Black

Cause of Death:

Mostly natural causes

Secondary: Suicide, force-related, pending investigations

Age Range: Deaths peak in late 50s to early 60s

Facility Type: Majority occur in state prisons (~16k)

Highlights
Live Matching: Syncs press releases with federal counts

Real-Time Alerts: Dashboards detect overdose spikes

Policy Impact: Supports reforms in death reporting transparency

Challenges
Missing or delayed records from certain states

Variability in terminology for causes of death

Inconsistent data formats and incomplete datasets

Limited resources in some jurisdictions

Deliverables
Merged and cleaned datasets

Statistical analysis and visualizations

Interactive map of prison deaths across U.S. states

Publicly accessible dashboards and policy tools

Next Steps
